The photograph is the product, as far as the buyer is concerned. It is worth five minutes per item, and it does not require anything you do not already own.

## What you need

- A phone from the last few years
- A window
- A large sheet of plain white paper, a white bedsheet, or a plain wall
- Optionally, a second sheet of white paper or card to bounce light back

That is the entire list. No lamp, no tripod, no camera.

## The light

**Use daylight, near a window, with the light coming from the side.** Morning or late afternoon is best; midday sun through a window is harsh.

- Never use the phone flash. It flattens the product and produces a hard shadow behind it.
- Do not shoot under a tube light or a yellow bulb at night. Colours come out wrong, and colour complaints are a common reason for refused parcels.
- If one side of the product is too dark, stand a white sheet of paper on that side to bounce light back into the shadow.

## The background

Plain, light, and the same for every product. A white or light grey background makes the product the only thing in the frame and makes your whole catalogue look consistent.

Avoid: patterned bedsheets, your shop counter, a carpet, anything with writing on it, and other products in the background. These make new listings look like second-hand goods.

## The shots to take

Five photographs per product, in this order:

1. **The main shot.** The whole product, straight on, filling most of the frame, on the plain background.
2. **An angle.** Three-quarter view, so the buyer can see depth.
3. **A detail.** Fabric texture, stitching, the port on a device, the closure on a bag.
4. **Scale.** The product next to something everyday — a hand, a phone, a water bottle. This prevents the single most common return: "it is much smaller than I thought".
5. **What is included.** Everything that comes in the box, laid out together.

If a product has variants, photograph each colour. Do not reuse one colour's photograph and mention the difference in the text; buyers do not read it and the parcel comes back.

## Settings and editing

- Clean the lens first. Phone lenses live in pockets and it makes a visible difference.
- Tap the product on screen to focus before you shoot.
- Shoot in landscape or square, not portrait, and leave a little space around the product so it can be cropped.
- Editing means cropping and a small brightness lift. Nothing else.
- **Do not increase the saturation.** A colour that looks richer on screen than in the parcel is the fastest route to a refused delivery.

## What gets a listing rejected

- Photographs taken from another website, a brand's catalogue, or a competitor's listing. Aside from being someone else's property, buyers recognise them and trust the listing less.
- Watermarks, phone numbers, price stickers or "WhatsApp me" text written on the image.
- Collages of several products in one image.
- Blurred, dark, or heavily filtered images.

A plain, sharp, honest photograph taken on a phone by a window outperforms a borrowed professional one, because it is the product that will actually arrive.
