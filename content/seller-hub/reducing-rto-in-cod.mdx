Cash on delivery is how most of Pakistan buys online, and the price of that is RTO: the buyer can refuse the parcel at the door at no cost to themselves. For a seller, it is usually the largest controllable cost in the business.

## What RTO actually costs

A refused parcel is not just a lost sale. Count it properly:

- The outbound shipping you already paid
- The RTO charge on the return leg
- Your packaging materials
- The goods tied up in transit for a week instead of being sold
- Handling the parcel twice

A Rs. 2,000 order that comes back can easily cost Rs. 300 to Rs. 400 in real terms. At a 25% RTO rate — not unusual for a seller who does nothing about it — that quietly consumes most of the margin on the orders that did succeed.

The encouraging part: RTO is highly reducible. Most of it comes from four or five causes, and all of them are addressable.

## Why parcels get refused

In rough order of frequency:

1. **It was not what they expected.** Colour differed, size ran small, the material felt cheaper than the photograph implied, or an accessory they assumed was included was not.
2. **They forgot they ordered it.** Impulse orders placed late at night, arriving five days later.
3. **They do not have the cash.** Nobody at home has the amount on the day.
4. **They ordered the same thing elsewhere** and whichever arrives first wins.
5. **Nobody was home**, or the phone was off, across all delivery attempts.
6. **The address was incomplete** — very common outside the major cities.

Only the first of these is under your direct control, but you can influence most of the rest.

## Fix the listing

This is where the largest reduction comes from, and it costs nothing.

- **Photograph the real product**, in daylight, with no saturation boost. A colour that looks richer on screen than in the box produces a refusal at the door.
- **State measurements in numbers**, not "medium". Medium means different things to different buyers.
- **List what is not included.** One line. It prevents a surprising share of refusals.
- **Disclose the flaw.** If a shirt runs small, if a colour varies by batch, if the box has a scuff — say so. A buyer who was warned keeps the parcel.
- **Add a scale photograph.** "Much smaller than I thought" is one of the most common refusal reasons, and one photograph next to a hand or a phone removes it.

## Confirm before dispatch

A short confirmation message before the parcel leaves is the single highest-return habit in this business.

Send it on WhatsApp, in Urdu, within a few hours of the order:

> Assalam-o-alaikum. Your order [number] for [product, colour, size] is ready to send. Total Rs. [amount], payable in cash at delivery. Confirm and we will dispatch today.

What this does:

- Catches the wrong colour or size *before* you pay for shipping
- Reminds the buyer they ordered, which removes the "I forgot" refusals
- Tells them the exact cash amount, so it is ready on the day
- Surfaces the buyers who were never serious, at a cost of one message rather than a full delivery cycle

Sellers who do this consistently report the largest single drop in RTO. If a buyer does not reply within 24 hours, message once more, then cancel rather than dispatch. A cancelled order costs you nothing; a refused one costs you the round trip.

## Manage the delivery window

- **Dispatch the same day or the next day.** RTO rises with every day between order and arrival. Two-day delivery is refused far less than six-day delivery.
- **Pass the buyer's alternative number** into the order notes if you have one. A parcel is refused after a failed contact attempt, not after a failed delivery attempt.
- **Check incomplete addresses before dispatch.** If the address has no house number, no street and no landmark, message and ask. Two minutes now against a round trip later.
- **Flag high-value orders.** For anything above your comfort threshold, insist on a confirmed reply before dispatching, or offer prepayment at a small discount.

## Measure it

You cannot manage what you do not count. Every week, record:

```
RTO rate = parcels returned / parcels dispatched
```

Then break it down by **product**, by **city**, and by **order value**. Almost every seller who does this finds the problem is concentrated rather than general:

- One product with a misleading photograph
- One city where addresses are consistently incomplete
- Orders above a certain value, where buyers hesitate at the door

Fix the concentration and the overall rate falls. A seller who cuts RTO from 25% to 10% has usually done more for their profit than one who doubled their traffic.
