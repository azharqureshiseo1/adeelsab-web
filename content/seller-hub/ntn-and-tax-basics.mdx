> This is general guidance written to help you ask the right questions. It is not tax advice, and the rules change. For anything specific to your situation, speak to a tax practitioner or the FBR helpline.

## The short answer

**You can register as a seller and start selling without an NTN.** You are not blocked.

But whether you have one affects how much withholding tax is deducted from your payouts, and being on the Active Taxpayer List means a materially lower rate. For most sellers doing any real volume, registering pays for itself quickly.

## What an NTN is

The National Tax Number is your registration number with the Federal Board of Revenue. For an individual in Pakistan, your **CNIC number is your NTN** once you have registered with the FBR — you are not issued a separate number. For a company or an AOP, a distinct NTN is issued.

Having an NTN does not by itself mean you owe tax. It means you are registered, you file a return each year, and your income is on the record. If your income is below the taxable threshold, you file and pay nothing.

## The Active Taxpayer List

This is the part that affects your money directly.

The FBR publishes the Active Taxpayer List (ATL), updated regularly. It contains everyone who has filed their return for the relevant year. Tax is withheld at source on payments to sellers, and **the rate applied to somebody on the ATL is significantly lower than the rate applied to somebody who is not**.

That withholding is not an extra cost in the long run — it is credited against your annual tax liability when you file, and refunded if you overpaid. But if you never file, you never claim it back, so for a non-filer it is simply money gone.

Two practical consequences:

- Being a filer increases what actually lands in your account on every payout.
- Filing once is not enough. The ATL is based on the most recent return, so you have to file every year to stay on it.

Your withholding appears as its own line on your settlement statement, against the order it relates to.

## How to register

Registration is free and is done online. You do not need an agent, though many people use one.

1. Go to the FBR IRIS portal at **iris.fbr.gov.pk**.
2. Choose **Registration for Unregistered Person**.
3. Enter your CNIC, a mobile number registered in your own name, and an email address. Both must be in your own name — this is checked.
4. Complete the verification codes sent to your phone and email.
5. Log in and complete the **Form 181** registration: personal details, your business address, and your business activity. For an online seller the activity is usually retail trade or wholesale, depending on what you do.
6. Submit. Registration is usually processed within a few working days.

After registering, you file an income tax return once a year. The return period for individuals typically runs to 30 September for the preceding tax year, though the deadline is sometimes extended.

To check whether you are currently on the ATL, use the FBR's online ATL status check with your CNIC, or send your CNIC by SMS to **9966**.

## What records to keep

Keep these from the first day, not from the day someone asks:

- **Your settlement statements.** Every payout, with the deductions itemised. These are your record of gross sales and tax already withheld.
- **Purchase invoices** for the stock you sell. Your taxable income is profit, not turnover — without purchase records, you cannot demonstrate your cost.
- **Expense records**: packaging, shipping you absorbed, rent and utilities for a business premises, transport.
- **Your bank or wallet statements** for the account that receives your payouts.

A simple monthly folder or spreadsheet is enough. The sellers who find filing painful are the ones reconstructing a year of records in September.

## What about sales tax?

Income tax and sales tax are different things. Sales tax registration is a separate process with its own threshold and its own monthly filing obligation, and whether it applies depends on what you sell and your turnover. Some categories are zero-rated or exempt; others are not.

If your turnover is growing into that territory, this is the point at which paying a tax practitioner for an hour of their time is genuinely worth it. Getting sales tax wrong is considerably more expensive than getting income tax wrong.
