Reselling is the most accessible way into online selling in Pakistan, because it requires no stock and no capital. It is also the one with the most unrealistic expectations attached to it.

## What reselling is

You sell somebody else's product to your own customer. The stock stays with the seller, who packs and ships it. You never handle the goods and never pay for them. Your income is the margin agreed between you and the seller.

It is not dropshipping, though the two get conflated. A dropshipper typically runs their own storefront and integrates with a supplier's systems. A reseller usually sells through channels they already have — a WhatsApp group, a Facebook page, an Instagram account, a physical counter with regular customers.

## What you need

Honestly, only two things:

**People who already listen to you.** A WhatsApp group of 200 people who buy from you, a Facebook page with engaged followers, or a shop where customers ask you for things you do not stock. This is the entire asset. Without an audience, reselling is just advertising to strangers with somebody else's margin.

**Time to answer messages and follow up orders.** Reselling is a communication business, not an inventory business.

You do not need money, storage space, a company, or a website.

## Where the margin comes from

The seller sets a margin they are willing to pay for a sale they would not otherwise have made. Typical ranges in Pakistan, very roughly:

| Category | Typical reseller margin |
|---|---|
| Fashion and apparel | 10 – 20% |
| Beauty and cosmetics | 12 – 25% |
| Home and kitchen | 8 – 15% |
| Mobile and electronics | 3 – 8% |

Electronics look unattractive on percentage and are often fine in absolute terms — 5% of Rs. 40,000 beats 20% of Rs. 1,200. Judge margin in rupees per sale, not in percent.

Be sceptical of anyone promising 40% or more on a commodity product. Either the base price is inflated, in which case it will not sell, or the margin is not real.

## The problem with informal deals

Most reselling in Pakistan is arranged over WhatsApp: you message a wholesaler, agree a rate, send them orders. It works until it does not, and it usually fails in one of three ways.

**The margin changes after the sale.** You quoted a customer based on one rate; the seller applies another after delivery.

**Payment is slow or partial.** You have no leverage and no record. Your only options are asking repeatedly and stopping.

**Stock runs out silently.** You sell something that is gone, and it is your customer who is disappointed, not the seller's.

None of this is because wholesalers are dishonest. It is because nothing was written down, and a verbal margin is not a record.

This is why a published-margin model matters. When the seller states inside the listing itself that resellers may sell it and at what margin, the terms exist before the sale rather than being negotiated after it. Neither side can quietly change them, and payment follows the same settlement rules as any other order. It removes the three failure modes above by removing the informality that causes them.

## Choosing products

Most new resellers pick what they personally like. That is the wrong filter. Better ones:

**Does my audience already buy this?** If your group is women buying unstitched fabric, mobile accessories will not sell to them no matter how good the margin.

**Can I answer questions about it?** Buyers ask about fit, material and compatibility. If you cannot answer, you lose the sale or generate a return.

**Is the margin worth the effort per sale?** Rs. 80 per sale means a hundred sales for a meaningful month. Rs. 600 per sale means fifteen.

**Is the seller reliable?** A great margin on a seller who ships late produces angry customers who stop buying from *you*. The seller's reliability is your reputation.

Start with five to ten products you understand well, not fifty you do not.

## Where resellers lose money

**Promising delivery times you do not control.** You are not shipping it. Quote the platform's published timeline, never your own optimistic estimate.

**Absorbing returns you did not budget for.** Understand who bears the cost when a parcel is refused before you start, not after the first one.

**Selling on price alone.** If your only pitch is being cheapest, someone with more margin to give away will undercut you next week.

**Neglecting the customer after the sale.** The sale is not the asset; the relationship is. A reseller with 300 repeat customers earns more than one who reaches 10,000 people once.

**Treating it as passive income.** It is not. It is a real job with no inventory cost.

## Is it worth it?

For somebody who already has an audience and no capital, reselling is one of the few genuinely low-risk ways to start. Your downside is your time. Your upside is capped by your audience size and your margin, which is why serious resellers spend more effort growing and keeping an audience than hunting for products.

It is a real business, run properly. It is not a way to make money without work, and any pitch suggesting otherwise is selling you something.
